Ruling on keyword-stuffing as research
Keep it as the 4th research entry. Your reasoning is sound and it's defensible.
The integrity test for sourceType: "research" isn't "the paper reports a positive lift number" — it's "the paper supports the recommendation we're making." A paper that explicitly identifies Keyword Stuffing as non-performing supports a "don't do this" recommendation just as rigorously as a paper identifying Statistics Addition as top-performing supports a "do this" recommendation. Both are primary-source-backed claims about what works in generative-engine optimization. The asymmetry would only matter if our schema treated sourceType: "research" as implying "positive lift expected," which it doesn't — expectedLift: null is exactly how we represent the absence of a quantified effect, regardless of direction.
The framing in the notes field handles the nuance correctly: "negative-signal removal rather than positive-lift intervention." That's honest and it's the right framing for the UI to inherit when commit 4 renders the badge.
One small refinement: the notes field says "in some configurations it slightly decreases it" — make sure that hedge ("in some configurations," "slightly") accurately reflects what the paper says rather than softening a clearer finding. If the paper is unambiguous that Keyword Stuffing reduces visibility, say so plainly. If the paper is genuinely mixed, the current hedge is fine. Re-check this one line against the paper text.
